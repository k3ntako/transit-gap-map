Walking distance to the train in New York City

The map opens on where people live more than a 10-minute walk from the nearest subway, Staten Island Railway, LIRR or Metro-North station. An area is darker the more people live there and the farther past 10 minutes they are. Walks follow streets and paths rather than a straight line. Each area is a census tract. Other layers show walk time alone, the wait for a train, access to jobs, and the same walk counting only stations with a step-free route. Hover an area or station for details.

Color areas by

At 0, every resident counts equally. Raising it gives more weight to car-free and lower-income households. The default is 0 because, when tested, weighting lowered agreement with the rail corridors agencies have proposed.

Areas past the 10-minute standard, grouped into zones of neighboring areas and shaded by each zone's total population. A rail line serves a strip of neighborhoods, so a zone is closer to what a project could serve than a single area. Zones stop at water.

The two inputs to the score, cut into thirds and crossed. It separates them, which the score cannot: a far area is not always an empty one. It also groups rather than ranks, so every dense-and-far area shares one color whatever its magnitude, and it is the one layer whose colors reverse between light and dark mode.

One center per borough, plus the stations that add the most jobs no other center covers. Chosen by where jobs are, not by ridership.

The same walk, counting only stations with a step-free route built. It is a lens and not part of the score: a station without an elevator is a case for an elevator, not for a new rail line.

The same trip by bike rather than on foot, over every street. It closes the middle of the gap and barely touches the worst of it, which is the point: the areas with the longest walks are far by street network, not just far on foot.

Show on map

One hypothetical station at a time, on a 400 m lattice, each circle sized by the excess walking it would remove. Nobody has proposed these: they are what the map says, not what an agency says, and they count in no figure on this page.

Filters

Filtered areas lose their color but keep their outline and details on hover, and still count in every figure.
